# Field vocabularies

Reference for [`fitness-audit`](SKILL.md), reached when the user's field has its own words for this analysis. Use their terms; the seven constituents are the same analysis renamed.

## Sociology / institutions

Functional analysis: **manifest vs latent function**, dysfunction, unanticipated consequences, **scope conditions**, **functional alternatives / equivalents**, equifinality. Modern descendants: analytical sociology (mechanism + scope conditions), field theory.

Caution: classical functionalism's teleology is discredited — no system "needs" its parts. Modern work treats mechanism and scope conditions as the load-bearing parts.

## Behavioural economics / policy

Mechanism, boundary conditions, **external validity**, **transportability**, site-selection bias. Instrument vocabularies: COM-B / Behaviour Change Wheel, the BCT taxonomy, Hood's NATO typology.

The inversion: there is no notion of a *proper* function, only effect against a counterfactual. "What is it for?" sits downstream of "compared to what?", and the constraints belong to the sample and setting rather than to the thing.

## Systems / requirements engineering

System definition, boundary and environment, interfaces, domain analysis, **ATAM** trade study, operational profile. The boundary question is settled here by declaration, which is why "who drew it" is answerable.

## Philosophy of technology

Artifact analysis: **proper function**, **use plan**, environment (Kroes & Houkes) — a technical object is only complete with the plan for its use and the setting that plan assumes. Also elenchus (test the claim against its own commitments) and hermeneutic part–whole (read the component through the whole it serves, then the whole through the component).
